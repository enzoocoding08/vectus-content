Repeating ten identical problems can make you fast at the steps. But exams rarely tell you which method comes next.

Try mixed practice:

1. Learn each method with a few focused examples.
2. Mix two or three related problem types.
3. Before solving, name the clue and choose the method.
4. Solve without looking at the example.
5. Explain why that method fits.

The goal is not difficulty for its own sake. It is practising the decision your exam will require: which approach works here?

Start with a small mix. If a concept is completely new, learn it first—then interleave it with related material.

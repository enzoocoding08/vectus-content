## Slide 1

Why Highlighting Feels Useful Quick study fix

It gives instant fluency and focus—but often not deeper learning.

## Slide 2

The fluency illusion 

Seeing highlighted text increases familiarity; familiarity is mistaken for mastery.

## Slide 3

Recognition vs. recall 

Highlighting supports recognition—later you recognize lines, but you can't produce the idea from memory.

## Slide 4

Selective attention trap 

We tend to highlight vivid phrases or examples, not the underlying principle or how to use it.

## Slide 5

Highlights become decorations 

Without testing, highlights turn into passive ornaments you reread instead of retrieving information.

## Slide 6

Make highlights into cues 

Highlight one line per idea; write a short question in the margin; later cover text and answer from memory.

## Slide 7

Takeaway: Use highlights smartly 

Highlights feel useful because they increase fluency. To learn, turn them into retrieval cues and practice active recall with spacing.
